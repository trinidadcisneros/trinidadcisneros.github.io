### COHS Calendar Year 2025 Changes
Base Data Adjustments/Program Changes include: (additional detail for rate development can be located in the CY 2025 Rate Certification):

* Long Term Care (LTC) Fee Schedule Changes - Adjustment to take into account the annual LTC rate changes. This adjustment accounts for pricing pressures based on fee-for-service rate increases that managed care plans are required to align with as well as considerations for SB 525 wage increases for these providers. The LTC Skilled Nursing Facility Work Force quality rates are reflected effective 1/1/2024.
* Hospice Fee Schedule Increase - Adjustment to account for the increase to the hospice services rates, and hospice room and board rates; effective October and August, respectively, each year. This adjustment accounts for pricing pressures based on fee-for-service rate increases that managed care plans are anticipated, but not required, to pay as well as considerations for SB 525 wage increases for these providers.
* Ground Emergency Medical Transportation (GEMT) Quality Assurance Fee (QAF) - SB 523 established the GEMT QAF program resulting in a per trip rate increase for GEMT service providers.
* GEMT AB 1705 - AB 1705 established the Public Provider GEMT (PP-GEMT) program resulting in a per trip rate increase for GEMT public service providers.
* AB 97 Buybacks - Effective January 1, 2023, Medi-Cal will be restoring the 10% AB 97 payment reductions previously applied for the following provider types that will now be exempt from AB 97 payment reductions:
      * Podiatrists
      * Prosthetists
* Community Health Worker - Effective July 1, 2022, this adjustment quantifies the impact of adding skilled and trained Community Health Workers who are able to provide non-ECM enrollees preventive health services in managed care systems.
* Dyadic Behavioral Health - Effective January 1, 2023, this adjustment quantifies the impact of offering new dyadic services (adding behavioral health treatment to routine pediatric care, for children age 0–20) and general behavioral health integration services, along with changes to a variety of existing services, meant to improve the health care of children by addressing developmental and behavioral health concerns as soon as they are identified.  
* SNF Directed Payment - Skilled Nursing Facility services for members in Two‑Plan/Geographic Managed Care (non-CCI) counties are priced consistent with State directed payment policy to reflect the payment amounts those providers would have received in the FFS delivery system.
* Population Transitions - Certain populations identified will be mandatorily transitioned from FFS to Managed Care delivery systems in stages with varying effective dates. Program Change adjustments were specifically made for the following populations transitioning from FFS to Managed Care:
    * Effective January 1, 2022:
        * Aid Code Populations:
            * Trafficking and Crime Victims Assistance Program, excluding Share of Cost members
            * Accelerated Enrollment
            * Child Health and Disability Prevention Infant Deeming
            * Pregnancy-related Medi-Cal
            * Breast and Cervical Cancer Treatment Program
    * Effective January 1, 2023:
        * Members Transitioning from FFS to Managed Care Who Were Previously Subject to Managed Care Transition
    * Effective January 1, 2024:
        * Undocumented 26 to 49 Expansion
        * Foster Care in new COHS counties
    * Effective January 1, 2025:
        * Foster Care in Single Plan counties
* Transitional Care Services - Effective January 1, 2023, increased Transitional Care Services were required for all high-risk members discharged from inpatient or LTC, including pregnant individuals, CCS, and Specialty Mental Health. Effective January 1, 2024, increased Transitional Care Services are required for both low-risk and high-risk members discharged from inpatient and LTC services.
* Targeted Rate Increase (TRI) - Effective January 1, 2024, this adjustment accounts for the increase of provider rates to at least 87.5 percent of Medicare for primary care (including nurse practitioners, physician assistants, and podiatrists), maternity care included OB/GYNs and doulas, and non-specialty mental health services. The adjustment is broken out into three categories below for the MCP's reference.
* TRI - Historical Proposition 56 - This adjustment accounts for historical Proposition 56 Physician supplemental payments now being funded through the base rates for eligible providers in TRI. Costs for these codes billed at an FQHC are budgeted within the FQHC Parity Adjustment as FQHCs were not previously subject to these supplemental payments. In addition, there are no supplemental payment amounts for members with a Full-Dual or Part B Only Partial-Dual status as these members have been historically excluded from this supplemental payment. While non-eligible providers will not be considered within the rate adjustment, plans may choose to make these payments, and these will be eventually reflected within plan's base data.
* TRI - FQHC Parity - This adjustment accounts for TRI's impact on the health plan obligation to pay FQHC providers similar to other Managed Care providers. In total, the FQHC Parity adjustment should not change the total PPS rates paid to FQHCs.
* TRI - All Other Increases - This amount reflects new funding to the mainstream capitation rates due to the TRI minimum directed payment. 
* Children and Youth Behavioral Health Initiative - Effective January 1, 2024, for LEA’s participating in Cohort 1, July 1, 2024 for LEA’s participating in Cohort 2, January 1, 2025, for LEA’s participating in Cohort 3, and July 1, 2025 for LEA’s participating in Cohort 4, this adjustment estimates the impact of additional behavioral health services in schools for children and youth age 0-25.
* Wellness Coach -The intent of the Wellness Coach benefit is to service the 0-25 population, but is technically available for all adults.
* Major Organ Transplant - Effective January 1, 2022, MOT became a managed care covered benefit in GME, Regional, Single-plan, and Two-Plan counties. This adjustment accounts for MOT ramp up that is not yet fully reflected in the base data used for CY 2025 rates.
* Medicare Part A Buy-In - Effective January 1, 2025, DHCS will pay the Medicare Part A premium for eligible Medicaid beneficiaries. Upon this change, the eligibility information for the qualified members within the SPD-LTC COA group will become reflective of SPD-LTC/Full-Dual COA group characteristics. This adjustment is to account for the movement of members from SPD-LTC to SPD-LTC/Full-Dual, as well as the discount in costs associated with these members that will now be covered by Medicare Part A.

Rate Adjustments include: (additional detail for rate development can be located in the CY 2025 Rate Certification): 

* Managed Care Adjustment - This adjustment accounts for anticipated changes in existing utilization and costs for the WCM COA due to contracting changes related to the CY 2024 procurement in the San Benito and Rural Upper Central regions.
* Population Acuity Adjustment - The population acuity adjustment accounts for material shifts in Medi-Cal Managed Care enrollment, primarily driven by the public health emergency unwinding, that would result in a change in the acuity level of that population. The intent of this adjustment is to better align health plan capitation payment to risk.

Rates do not include the impact of:
* American Indian Health payments
* Behavioral Health Treatment, Maternity, and Hepatitis C supplemental payments
* Blood factor pharmaceuticals were carved out of rates effective July 1, 2014
* Health Insurance Providers Fee
* Hospital Quality Assurance Fee pass-through payments
* Managed Care Organization Tax, Senate Bill 2 (Chapter 2, Statutes 2015-16 Second Extraordinary Session)
* Managed Care Private Hospital directed payments
* Managed Care Public Hospital directed payments
* Physician services supplemental payments, Assembly Bill 120 (Chapter 22, Statutes of 2017)

### COHS Calendar Year 2023 Changes
Through communication with CMS, DHCS is required to set capitation rates for the UIS and SIS populations separately. Further, the capitation rates for the UIS population are required to be separated by federally eligible services (namely, pregnancy-related and emergency services) and services paid by the State alone (all other services). Within the rates being certified within this certification, the UIS and SIS populations are separated. Further, the SIS population capitation rates are being certified for all components while only the federally eligible rate component is being certified for the UIS population. The split of the UIS and SIS populations occurs within the base data, and capitation rates are developed after this split occurs for both populations.

* The pregnancy and emergency rates displayed above are subsets of the UIS Federal rate.

* Non-Medical Transportation (NMT) -	NMT is non-emergent transportation to medical services for beneficiaries where the mode of transportation has no medical component. NMT includes, but is not limited to, transportation for beneficiaries by modes of transportation such as passenger car, taxicab, public transportation, and mileage reimbursement. It does not include transportation of the sick, injured, invalid, convalescent, inform, or otherwise incapacitated members who need to be transported by ambulances, litter vans, or wheelchair vans. Effective July 1, 2017, NMT became a managed care covered service for transportation to medically necessary services included in the managed care contract. Effective October 1, 2017, NMT will be a managed care covered service for all Medi-Cal services, including those not covered under the managed care contract (which includes, but is not limited to, specialty mental health, substance use disorder, dental, and any benefit covered through Medi-Cal fee-for-service).

Doula services include personal support to women and families throughout a woman's pregnancy, childbirth, and postpartum experience. This includes emotional and physical support, provided during pregnancy, labor, birth, and the postpartum period.

Through communication with CMS, DHCS is required to set capitation rates for the UIS and SIS populations separately. Further, the capitation rates for the UIS population are required to be separated by federally eligible services (namely, pregnancy-related and emergency services) and services paid by the State alone (all other services). Within the rates being certified within this certification, the UIS and SIS populations are separated. Further, the SIS population capitation rates are being certified for all components while only the federally eligible rate component is being certified for the UIS population.
